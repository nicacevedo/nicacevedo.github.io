Research and Industry Experience

Research Assistant · MIT Operations Research CenterSept. 2024–present
  • Fair machine learning (first-author). Developed two covariance-based LightGBM training objectives, with custom gradients and Hessians, to reduce regressivity in property valuation: a direct squared-covariance penalty and an observation-additive surrogate. GitHub.
  • Results. The cross-validated surrogate reduced the magnitude of price-related bias (PRB) by 49% on held-out sales and 39% on 2025 sales versus an unpenalized fit, with price-scale R² essentially unchanged and log-price RMSE up 3.1% and 1.7%, respectively. Data: 382,897 historical Cook County sales, a separate 26,641-sale 2025 test set and an eight-county benchmark, where both methods reduced regressivity in every county.
  • Research adoption. Collaborated with the Cook County Assessor’s Office, which merged the direct covariance penalty into its open-source residential valuation model as a selectable training objective (mse_cov; PR #475, July 2026).
  • Numerical optimization on GPUs. Studied the convergence and numerical accuracy of large-scale first-order linear programming (LP) solvers on GPUs; implemented and tested iterative refinement and residual-based matrix scaling in a fork of cuPDLP.jl (CUDA.jl). GitHub.
  • Data-center modeling. Developing optimization and public-data models of data-center operations, including workload scheduling, electricity use and cooling-water use. GitHub.
  • Tabular deep learning. Built fairness-aware PyTorch networks with projection layers and group-balanced batching, and compared them with gradient boosting under temporal cross-validation.
Researcher · Web Intelligence Centre, Universidad de Chile / ACHSDec. 2023–Apr. 2024
  • Built department-level patient-volume forecasts for Hospital del Trabajador (Prophet, NeuralProphet; random-forest and XGBoost baselines) and used them in a scheduling optimization model for appointment capacity planning.
Research Assistant · FCFM, Universidad de ChileAug. 2022–July 2024
  • Master’s thesis. Reformulated LASSO feature selection as a second-order cone program and developed a column-generation decomposition driven by conic-dual pricing. On large, strongly regularized instances with sparse solutions, it solved in seconds problems on which the original SOCP and coordinate-descent LASSO needed over 30 minutes, and it converged faster than coordinate descent and Frank–Wolfe on highly correlated features. Advisors: Fernando Ordonez, Renaud Chicoisne.
